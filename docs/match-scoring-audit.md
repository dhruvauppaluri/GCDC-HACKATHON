# Match scoring audit: Simplify internship trial

This is an engineering audit of **documented qualification coverage**, not a claim that the score predicts hiring outcomes. The tested candidate is the sanitized Dhruva trial profile. The tested role is Reliable Robotics' FPGA Design Engineer Intern listing from SimplifyJobs. Test credentials and API keys were kept outside the repository.

## What caused the 80-to-60 change

The old job parser searched the entire posting for skill words. It turned `VHDL, Verilog, or SystemVerilog` and `C, C++, or Python` into multiple separate mandatory skills. It also treated `testing` from an accommodation paragraph as a job requirement and failed to classify FPGA industry experience as preferred. The old Judge then depended on whichever requirements the positive Lawyer happened to mention, with an additional 20-point strongest-citation bonus. Adding two repositories changed the Lawyer's long evidence input and its selected arguments. The observed score changed from 80/100 with one repo to 60/100 with three. This comparison varied both portfolio content and model output; it did not isolate a causal effect for each factor.

The posting actually lists three basic qualification groups, two preferred groups, and two eligibility conditions. One of the basic groups accepts **any one** of VHDL, Verilog, or SystemVerilog; another accepts **any one** of C, C++, or Python. The new extraction keeps those choices grouped, quotes the original posting for every criterion, and separates eligibility from scored qualifications. It rejects fabricated source quotes and abstains if the extraction fails. It runs on **Interested**, not for every four-hour feed refresh, and caches a validated result by JD, model, and extraction version.

## Revised scoring rule

- Required qualification groups have weight 2; preferred groups have weight 1. Eligibility is reported separately and never adds points.
- A group receives full documented coverage from a matching Depth-demonstrated skill with an inspected repository or document citation, half coverage from an explicit candidate claim, or zero when no support is shown. An `or` group is satisfied by one alternative. A listed equipment group without `or` needs at least two matching examples.
- The numeric score is the weighted average of those coverages, scaled to 0–100. It is deterministic for fixed source-checked requirements and evidence. Lawyer arguments remain visible as explanations and objections; repetition or an unsupported argument cannot change the score.
- `not_shown` means the supplied evidence does not demonstrate the criterion. It does not assert that the candidate lacks the skill. If the full JD or source-checked qualifications are unavailable, the Judge returns no numeric score and skips Lawyer model calls. The quick match treats words found in a linked JD as **signals**, not confirmed mandatory gaps.

The weights, grade bands, and half-credit for candidate claims are provisional. The implementation's confidence is low unless at least half of scored groups have inspected-artifact support; even then it is at most medium until human calibration.

## Checks completed

- The original Simplify-only load test covered 2,716 listings, 20 linked-page reads (13 usable, 7 unavailable), and 200 matching requests from four accounts during cache writes (zero errors; local test-client p95 95 ms).
- Three real, linked JDs were checked with Gemini qualification extraction: hardware test, robotics software research, and product strategy. One software response had nine eligibility alternatives; the validator now ignores eligibility alternatives rather than rejecting all valid qualifications. A transient Gemini HTTP 503 exposed the need for bounded 502/503/504 retries.
- A live Gemini Depth run analyzed all three GitHub repositories in the sanitized profile. For the FPGA posting, one-repository and three-repository fixed-evidence runs now both score **50/100**. Live independent Gemini Lawyers produced 6 versus 7 positive points and 5 versus 7 skeptical points; the score remained 50 in both runs.
- The revised authenticated API flow completed with all three saved Depth results, seven source-quoted JD criteria (three required, two preferred, two eligibility), two Gemini Lawyers, and the deterministic Judge. Reopening the same JD reused its cached qualification extraction. The score was 50/100: one required group had inspected-artifact support, two had candidate-claim support, and neither preferred group had documented support.
- The Lawyer input is now bounded to cited Depth anchors and up to four job-relevant uncited excerpts per project. The same three-repository case passed 79 evidence items (61 repository excerpts) instead of 174 items (156 repository excerpts); all three full Depth assessments remain attached to the case. One Gemini call returned HTTP 503 after retries; the review now reports an unavailable Lawyer side while still displaying the independently computed documented-coverage score. On rerun, both live Gemini Lawyers completed with 8 advocate and 3 skeptic points, and the score remained 50/100.
- Backend tests cover source-quote rejection, `or` grouping, eligibility separation, irrelevant-repository monotonicity, unsupported Lawyer-point resistance, unverified-JD abstention, cache reuse, and transient Gemini retries. Run `python -m pytest -q` from the repository root and `npm run build` from `web/`.

## Remaining accuracy limits

The source-quote check catches invented criteria but cannot detect an omitted real requirement or a misclassified responsibility. The exact-term evidence rule can miss semantically equivalent experience, especially communication, research, and PM work. Candidate résumé text and self-ratings remain claims; a repository shows implementation but does not prove personal authorship. Scores are **documented coverage**, not interview performance, hiring probability, or eligibility decisions. No human-labeled HW/CS/PM application set exists yet to estimate criterion precision, score calibration, or false-negative rates. Before using score bands beyond the local trial, Rishi and Harsh should label a stratified set of real JDs and candidate evidence, adjudicate disagreements, and compare the algorithm's per-criterion decisions and rank ordering to those labels. Include portfolio-size, track, JD-source, and missing-evidence slices.

This design responds to evidence that long-context models can miss relevant information in the middle of large inputs ([Liu et al., *Lost in the Middle*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)). Gemini's structured output documentation says schema compliance still requires semantic validation ([Google Gemini structured outputs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/structured-output)). The need to measure validity and reliability in context is consistent with [NIST's AI Risk Management Framework](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/3-sec-characteristics/). For actual employment selection, the [EEOC's selection-procedure guidance](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/employment-tests-and-selection-procedures) calls for job-related validation; this local feedback score has not undergone that validation.

## Model Judge handoff

The local integration now runs a third model agent after the two independent Lawyers. It receives both arguments, Paralegal claim signals, scored job criteria, the documented-coverage calculation, and only the cited evidence needed to adjudicate. Its recommendation, reasons, strengths, and concerns are validated against supplied requirement and evidence IDs. The model cannot alter the numeric score; if a Lawyer or Judge model call fails, the review says which stage did not finish. A compact live Gemini trial completed both Lawyers (four advocate and three skeptical points) and the Judge (`consider`) with a 67/100 evidence score. A separate three-repository run saw both Lawyer calls return HTTP 503, so the Judge agent was correctly skipped while the 50/100 documented-coverage score remained visible.

## Full browser run with two Gemini projects

On November 15, a fresh browser case used the active Simplify Reliable Robotics FPGA listing, read its linked JD, extracted seven source-quoted groups (three required, two preferred, two eligibility), and ran Gemini Depth on all three saved GitHub repositories. All three evaluations completed. The Paralegal emitted six hardware résumé claim signals; both independent Lawyers completed with eight advocate and five skeptical points; the Judge agent returned `consider`. This fresh evidence produced **62/100** documented coverage. The earlier 50/100 run used different saved Depth evaluations; scores are reproducible for fixed evidence, but model-generated Depth findings can vary across runs. The browser review was stored as complete with no Lawyer errors.

The browser run also exposed an eligibility wording defect: the Judge prose treated a candidate-reported graduation date as satisfying a condition while the formal eligibility field remained unknown. The integration now fixes the displayed and newly stored eligibility note to unverified whenever eligibility is unknown. Human review is still needed to assess factual strength wording and score calibration.
